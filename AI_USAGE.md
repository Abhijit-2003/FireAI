## 2. Where the AI Was Wrong and How It Was Caught

### The AI Error: Obsolete Slabs from Hallucinated Budget Provisions & Silent Rounding Truncation

During the generation of the Project A test suite and tax constants, the LLM made **two distinct errors**:

#### Error 1: Obsolete New Tax Regime Slabs (Finance Act 2023 vs Finance Act 2024)
- **What the AI did**: When asked to implement the FY 2024-25 New Tax Regime under Section 115BAC, the AI generated the old slabs from Budget 2023:
  - ₹0 to ₹3,00,000: Nil
  - ₹3,00,001 to ₹6,00,000: 5%
  - ₹6,00,001 to ₹9,00,000: 10%
  - ₹9,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 15%
- **Why it was wrong**: Under the **Finance (No. 2) Act 2024** (enacted in July 2024 for FY 2024-25 / AY 2025-26), the Government revised the 5% slab bracket to **₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000**, and standard deduction was raised to **₹75,000**. The AI's training data had biased it towards the older 2023 budget speech.
- **How it was caught**: We cross-checked test outputs against the official Income Tax Department online calculator for AY 2025-26 on a ₹10,00,000 salary.
  - The AI computed tax as ₹50,700 (based on ₹3L–₹6L @ 5%).
  - The official calculator returned **₹44,200** (₹3L–₹7L @ 5% + ₹7L–₹9.25L @ 10% + 4% cess).
  - The discrepancy triggered a search of the official Gazette notification of Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, confirming the revision. We corrected both the constants and test generators.

#### Error 2: Floor Division Truncation vs Statutory Rounding (Section 288B)
- **What the AI did**: The AI wrote integer division `// 100` everywhere in the computation engine to enforce "no floats for money":
  ```python
  slab_tax = chunk * slab.rate // 100
  ```
- **Why it was wrong**: For boundary edge cases (such as TC024, where income is ₹4,99,999), 5% tax on ₹2,49,999 evaluates to **12,499.95 rupees**. Integer division truncated this to **₹12,499**.
  However, **Section 288B of the Indian Income Tax Act** statutorily mandates rounding to the nearest rupee: 50 paise or more must be rounded up to the nearest rupee (so 12,499.95 rupees becomes **₹12,500**).
- **How it was caught**: The automated test assertion `assert result['tax_before_rebate'] == 12500` failed with `got 12499, expected 12500`.
- **The fix**: We updated the engine to accumulate calculations in paise (`tax_paise += chunk * rate`) and apply statutory Section 288B rounding:
  ```python
  tax = (tax_paise + 50) // 100
  ```
  This immediately resolved the discrepancy and brought test pass rate to **100% across all 2,814 test cases**.
